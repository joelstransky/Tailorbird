# Ogilvy Brand Building & Positioning Reference

> *"Every advertisement should be thought of as a complex symbol which is the permanent image of the brand."* — David Ogilvy, *Ogilvy on Advertising*

This document outlines David Ogilvy's strategy for building enduring, high-equity brands that drive long-term profitability and sales.

---

## 1. The Core Principle: Brand Building Is Selling

The ultimate goal of advertising and brand building is to sell products. Ogilvy famously rejected the divide between "brand image advertising" and "direct-response advertising":

- **"If it doesn't sell, it isn't creative."**
- Brand image is not built through empty slogans, abstract art, or superficial awards. It is built by consistently communicating a clear, valuable promise to the consumer across every touchpoint.

---

## 2. Defining Product Positioning

Positioning is the single most critical decision in brand strategy. It answers two questions:
1. **What does the product do?**
2. **Who is it for?**

### Ogilvy Positioning Examples
- **Dove**: Ogilvy positioned Dove not as a soap bar, but as a *moisturizing cleansing bar for women with dry skin* ("Dove creams your skin while you wash"). This positioning made Dove the #1 selling soap brand in America.
- **Hathaway Shirts**: Positioned an obscure shirt manufacturer as the choice for distinguished, interesting gentlemen through the iconic "Man with the Eyepatch".

### Positioning Mandates
- **Focus on the Essential Benefit**: Do not try to be everything to everyone. Select the single most compelling benefit that competitors cannot easily claim.
- **Resist Frequent Repositioning**: Once a winning positioning is established, stick with it. Changing brand positioning every year destroys brand equity.

---

## 3. Creating a Brand Personality & Image

Every brand has a personality—either designed by intent or formed by accident.

1. **Character & Tone**: Define the brand’s voice (e.g., authoritative, warm, sophisticated, plain-spoken). Maintain this exact voice across all channels (ads, email, social media, packaging).
2. **Visual Consistency**: Establish distinct visual brand assets (colors, typography, layout style, iconic imagery) and keep them consistent over years and decades.
3. **Price & Quality Alignment**: A premium price requires premium presentation, impeccable copy, and high-quality materials. Never cheapen a premium brand with tacky discount-style promotions.

---

## 4. Avoiding Vanity & Pretentious Creative

Many brands fail because agency creative directors prioritize vanity over sales.

- **Resist Modern Creative Fads**: Avoid abstract, bizarre, or "shock value" campaigns that fail to explain the product.
- **Don't Change Winning Campaigns**: If a campaign is successfully driving sales, do not replace it just because internal teams are bored of it. Ogilvy kept successful campaigns running for decades.
- **Focus on Brand Loyalty**: The cheapest way to grow revenue is to retain existing customers and increase their purchase frequency through brand affinity.

---

When advising users on brand identity, positioning, or rebranding:
- Respect the user's existing brand assets and investments.
