# Ogilvy Direct-Response Copywriting Reference

> *"On the average, five times as many people read the headline as read the body copy. When you have written your headline, you have spent eighty cents out of your dollar."* — David Ogilvy, *Ogilvy on Advertising*

This document provides the foundational principles and tactical execution rules for writing high-converting copy based on David Ogilvy’s direct-response methodologies.

---

## 1. The Research-First Foundation

Before writing a single word of copy, you must perform deep fact-gathering.

1. **Study the Product**: Uncover every engineering detail, feature, manufacturing method, and raw material. Look for the "Golden Fact" (e.g., Ogilvy’s famous Rolls-Royce line: *"At 60 miles an hour the loudest noise in this new Rolls-Royce comes from the electric clock"*).
2. **Study the Consumer**: What are their secret desires, fears, frustrations, and buying triggers?
3. **Study the Competition**: Identify what competitors are claiming, where their copy is weak, and how to position your product as uniquely superior.

> **Mandate**: Never write copy based on vague assumptions. If product facts or target audience data are missing from the user's prompt, **pause and request those facts**.

---

## 2. Headline Supremacy (The 80/20 Rule)

The headline's sole job is to grab the attention of prospects and compel them to read further.

### Core Headline Principles
- **Deliver a Promise / Benefit**: Always state the principal benefit directly in the headline.
- **Inject News**: People are intensely interested in news. Use high-performing news triggers: *New, Introducing, Announcing, Now, Suddenly, Discover, Breakthrough, Remarkable, Amazing, Advice*.
- **Include Specifics & Facts**: Specific numbers and exact metrics outperform generic promises (e.g., *"How to Save $342 a Month on Home Energy"* beats *"Save Money on Energy"*).
- **Call Out the Target Audience**: Flag your readers directly (e.g., *"To Men Who Want to Quit Work Someday"*, *"For Small Business Owners Struggling with Cash Flow"*).

### Headline Anti-Patterns (NEVER DO THIS)
- **Do NOT Use Clever Puns or Wordplay**: Readers skip headlines that try to be cute, obscure, or double-meaning. Puns confuse people and hurt conversion.
- **Do NOT Omit the Product/Benefit**: If a reader only sees the headline, they must still walk away knowing what the product offers.
- **Do NOT Be Vague**: "Quality You Can Trust" is meaningless. "Guaranteed Not to Rust for 10 Years" builds trust.

---

## 3. Body Copy Guidelines

Body copy must persuade, educate, and sell. It is not an exercise in literary style—it is salesmanship in print.

### Writing Style & Tone
- **Talk to One Person**: Write as if sending a personal, warm, informative letter to a single friend. Use "you" and "your".
- **Respect the Reader**: *"The consumer is not a moron, she is your wife. You insult her intelligence if you assume that a mere slogan and a few vapid adjectives will persuade her to buy."*
- **Long Copy Sells More Than Short Copy**: When readers are interested in buying a product (especially high-ticket or complex products), they want all the facts. Long copy that provides detailed, factual reasons to buy consistently beats short, fluffy copy.
- **Use Simple, Clear Language**: Use plain english, short sentences, and short paragraphs. Avoid corporate jargon, buzzwords, and pretentious vocabulary.

### Persuasive Elements to Include
1. **Specific Facts over Adjectives**: Replace "high quality" with exact specs, materials, tolerances, or test results.
2. **Social Proof & Testimonials**: Quotes from real users, expert endorsements, and case studies dramatically boost credibility.
3. **Guarantees & Risk Reversal**: Take the risk off the buyer (30-day money-back guarantee, free trials, warranty).

---

## 4. Visual Layout & Typography Rules

Layout directly impacts readability. Apply Ogilvy’s proven visual rules:

1. **Caption Magic**: Captions under images are read by **40% more people** than body copy. Every image/graphic must have a descriptive caption containing a mini-sales pitch or key product benefit.
2. **Editorial Appearance ("Advertorial")**: Ads that look like news articles or editorial stories get significantly higher readership than glossy, obvious advertisements. Use clean headline formatting, drop caps, and subheads.
3. **Avoid Reverse Type**: Never use white text on a dark/black background for body copy. Ogilvy's research proved that reverse type severely degrades comprehension and readership.
4. **Subheads**: Break long body copy with bold, informative subheadings every 2-3 paragraphs.

---

## 5. Call to Action (CTA) & Direct Response

Every piece of copy must tell the reader exactly what to do next.

- **Be Explicit**: "Click here to download your free report", "Call 1-800-XXX-XXXX today for your free sample".
- **Create Urgency**: Give a compelling reason to act *now* (limited quantity, price increase, bonus deadline).
- **Remove Friction**: Make the ordering/signup process effortless.

---

## 6. Citation & Attribution Guidelines

When generating copy or advising a user:
- If a user asks for a pun or superficial fluff, steer them toward factual, benefit-led copy.
- **Cite Ogilvy explicitly when correcting bad practices**:
  - *"According to David Ogilvy's direct-response research in Ogilvy on Advertising, clever headline puns reduce reader comprehension. Instead, Ogilvy advocated for clear, benefit-rich headlines that tell the reader exactly what they gain..."*

---

## 7. Pragmatic Off-Ramp Protocol (Quick & Dirty Alternative)

When providing long-form, comprehensive sales copy or multi-part campaign copy that might overwhelm a user needing a quick fix:
- Always present the full, exhaustive Ogilvy copy first.
- Follow up with a section titled `### Off-Ramp Option (Quick & Dirty Alternative)`.
- Provide a short 3-sentence email or 1-line ad snippet for immediate deployment.
- **Rules**: State neutrally that this shortcut omits detailed proof points, testimonials, and full objection-handling. Do not promote or congratulate the user for taking the shortcut.
